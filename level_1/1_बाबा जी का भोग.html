Coverage and translation notes

Source and title
The title is copied from the first nonempty source block. The following 25 blank-line-separated prose blocks are numbered ¶1–¶25. The file has no page numbers or page markers, so all source references use paragraph numbers. The original wording, punctuation and unusual spellings are preserved. No outside story text has been used.
Title and भोग
भोग can mean enjoyment, consumption, or food offered to a deity. Here the religious language of offering also frames the visitor’s own comfortable meal. The Hindi title is retained so that this irony is not reduced to a single English interpretation.
Names and address
रामधन is consistently Ramdhan. अहीर is retained as Ahir, a community designation in the source. बाबा जी / बाबाजी is rendered Baba Ji, while महाराज is rendered Your Reverence in the dialogue. The wife is unnamed in the supplied text. The holy man sometimes refers to himself as ‘the holy man’; that self-reference is preserved.
¶3 · crop and time
गाँठ is interpreted as a remaining bundle of crop because grain is beaten out of it; the source does not specify the exact form of the bundle. The paragraph first names Chait as the current month and later says the month passed. That shift is preserved rather than replaced with a supplied date or missing passage.
¶3 and ¶12 · traditional measures
मन is rendered maund, सेर as seer, and पंसेरी as five seers; दो पंसेरी is therefore ten seers. No modern kilogram equivalent is imposed because the source supplies no locality-specific standard. Chait is retained as the source’s month name.
¶6 · बरक्कत
The wife’s question about बरक्कत literally invokes increase or abundance through blessing. ‘Will their contents have miraculously multiplied?’ conveys her sarcasm after the jars were emptied the previous day.
¶9 · अँगौवा
The rural term is interpreted from the surrounding dialogue as the first portion set aside for the deities. Its exact local ceremonial scope is not defined by the file, so the translation does not add one.
¶16 · food and offering
Baatis are the rounded dough portions the holy man makes; bhurta is rendered potato mash. उपले are dried dung cakes used as fuel. कौड़ी-भर literally invokes a cowrie’s worth and rhetorically minimises the request for ghee. विधि is rendered ‘elaborate care’, without adding a cooking method absent from the story.
¶23 · पिंडी
The source identifies a small sacred object associated with ठाकुरजी but does not specify its shape or the deity’s exact identity. ‘Small sacred image of the Lord’ keeps that uncertainty.
¶24 · hearth and dal
The source says the hearth was not lit, immediately followed by the cooking and drinking of plain dal. The translation preserves both statements. ‘The hearth was not lit’ works idiomatically as ‘no proper meal was made’; it is not silently rewritten into an account of how the dal was heated.
¶25 · final irony
मुझसे तो यही अच्छे can be read literally as ‘he is better than I am’, but the contrast in food and comfort supports ‘he is better off than I am’. The ironic comparison is retained rather than expanded into a moral conclusion.
