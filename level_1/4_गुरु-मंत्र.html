कड़ककर हाँक लगाते हैंThey give a loud, stern, forceful shout. कड़ककर suggests a sharp or commanding tone; हाँक लगाना means to call out loudly.सन्त लोग द्वार पर जाते ही कड़ककर हाँक लगाते हैं, जिससे घर के लोग चौंक पड़ें और उत्सुक होकर द्वार की ओर दौड़ें।Moteram advises intimidation and a dramatic entrance so the household takes notice and offers food.¶1

This does not mean literally flying away with plates.¶1

Coverage and translation notes

Comic language
The satire depends on forms of address and deliberately nonstandard Hindi. Aap, tum and tu are kept in the explanation; ‘thee-self’ and awkward English grammar suggest the affected speech without changing the Hindi. The shift from ‘we’ to ‘I’ in the chosen cry is retained.
Cries and hymn
The nonsense-like rhyming cries are translated closely rather than supplied with an invented coherent meaning. The five verse paragraphs remain separate. The Dark One addresses the deity in the hymn. The intoxicants are part of the narrated satire, not added instruction.
Requested learning vocabulary
The requested 20 words and 15 expressions are explained in context. Published spellings such as नेवते, चमगादड़, खँजड़ी and गिड़-गिड़ाकर are retained. The fragment सदैव उचित is completed as सदैव उचित व्यवहार करते रहना and explained through the preceding forms of address माई and बाबा. Figurative expressions about food, the body, and pretence are distinguished from literal meanings.
