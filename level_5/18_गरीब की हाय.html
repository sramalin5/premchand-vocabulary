Coverage and translation notes

Satire and bitter “blessings”
The praise of Ramsevak’s respectability and his son’s conduct is ironic. In ¶11, wishing him well and offering blessings describes Moonga’s curses ironically. Nagin, literally a female cobra, is the narrator’s designation for the wife, not an independently supplied personal name.
Source readings
ब्रह्मा in ¶5 is read geographically as Burma in the military context. The source’s आगमन (“arrival”) in ¶33 is rendered as the predicament described by the surrounding scene. खाली in the cow-feed list in ¶34 is understood as खली, oilcake. These readings do not alter the reproduced Hindi.
Uncertainty and fear
The narrator attributes Nagin’s death to fear and presents the family’s visions as experiences of terror. The ascetic in ¶49 resembles Ramsevak, but the text does not explicitly identify him or state who set the house on fire. Neither uncertainty is filled in.
Historical social setting
The passage about ritual pollution and exclusion from services records the village’s caste customs. मुन्सिफी, मुख्तार, रेहनामा, and ताजीरात हिन्द are period legal terms; they are explained here without replacing the original wording.
Joined source words
The source prints तहकीकातकी without a space in ¶50. The Words table retains that exact printed form, meaning made an investigation.
