Coverage and translation notes

Names as printed
The rebel in ¶1 is printed शाहजहाँ लोदी, retained as Shah Jahan Lodi rather than replaced from historical memory. The queen is Kulina; Qadir Khan’s spelling is kept consistent despite variation in the Hindi typography.
Source irregularities
In ¶6 न्यासी normally means trustee, but the list of royal virtues suggests न्यायी, just; the translation follows that contextual reading. ¶21 prints “राजा रुक गए हैं” before recalling his prohibition; the translation reflects the prohibition explained immediately afterward. In ¶48 “मैं तुम्हें खिला सकती” lacks a negative even though the surrounding sentences reject the act; its literal affirmative sense is retained rather than silently repaired.
Weapons, numbers, and chronology
The source describes armed duels even while calling the contestants पहलवान. The large crowds and the report of hundreds dying during celebration are retained as printed. The return after a year and the hunting sequence are not rearranged.
Honor and the final scene
The speeches and narration express the story’s martial and gender ideals. Hardaul’s action and Kulina’s conflicting thoughts are translated in full. मूर्ति in ¶52 is rendered “image” or “form”; the source does not clarify the precise visual reference. The story ends with Jujhar Singh’s contradictory smile and tears, with no later events supplied.
