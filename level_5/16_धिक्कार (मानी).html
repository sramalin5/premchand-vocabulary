Coverage and translation notes

Social setting and speakers’ views
The story depicts the stigma and abuse directed at a young widow. Its insults, caste hierarchy, and Indranath’s qualified remarks about remarriage are preserved as the speakers’ views. Mani’s self-condemnation is not silently rewritten into the narrator’s judgment.
Different accounts of the disappearance
The passengers in ¶92 conjecture an accidental fall. The later telegram and Indranath’s account report that Mani jumped. The translation retains that sequence without making the earlier conjecture certain.
Source inconsistencies
Although ¶1 says Mani had been married, ¶24 says she had never seen her former husband’s face; both statements are retained. The wording recalled in ¶87 includes an injunction not quoted in the earlier station speech; the recollection is preserved as printed. Gokul’s promise in ¶39 shifts between “today” and “tomorrow,” and the source’s sequence is retained.
Railway and customary terms
A जनाना compartment was reserved for women; a मरदाना compartment was for men. Sixteen annas made a rupee, so सोलहों आना means completely. The title धिक्कार conveys condemnation or reproach.
