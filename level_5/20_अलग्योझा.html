Coverage and translation notes

Names and family address
Ragghu is Bhola’s son from the first marriage; Panna is his stepmother, whom he and the children frequently call काकी, rendered “Aunt.” The children call Ragghu दादा, meaning elder brother here. लछमन and लक्षमन are rendered consistently as Lachhman. रावत in ¶43 is retained as Rawat rather than silently replaced with Bhola.
Rural and financial readings
मुहर in ¶22 is a gold coin ornament worn at the neck. The five-rupee calf adjustment called मुजरा is terse in the source; the translation retains it as a credit without inventing transaction details. नारियल in the later scenes denotes a coconut-shell smoking pipe. A seer, kos, and bigha are customary units.
Narrative judgments and medicine
The narrator’s judgments about selfishness, family division, and women are preserved. Basant Malati is a named traditional medicine in the narrative, not a treatment recommendation.
Source irregularities and ending
¶148 prints “सोने और ईट,” read in context as gold bricks. ¶188 prints शौक where the context indicates शोक, sorrow. The source’s elapsed-year and age references are retained without adjustment. The ending reveals mutual interest but does not narrate a wedding.
