¶1 रात 'भक्तमाल' पढ़ते-पढ़ते न जाने कब नींद आ गयी। कैसे-कैसे महात्मा थे जिनके लिए भगवत्-प्रेम ही सबकुछ था, इसी में मग्न रहते थे। ऐसी भक्ति बड़ी तपस्या से मिलती है। क्या मैं यह तपस्या नहीं कर सकती? इस जीवन में और कौन-सा सुख रखा है? आभूषणों से जिसे प्रेम हो वह जाने, यहाँ तो इनको देखकर आँखें फूटती हैं; धन-दौलत पर जो प्राण देता हो वह जाने, यहाँ तो इसका नाम सुनकर ज्वर-सा चढ़ आता है। कल पगली सुशीला ने कितनी उमंगों से मेरा शृंगार किया था, कितने प्रेम से बालों में फूल गूँथे थे। कितना मना करती रही, न मानी। आखिर वही हुआ जिसका मुझे भय था। जितनी देर उसके साथ हँसी थी, उससे कहीं ज्यादा रोयी। संसार में ऐसी भी कोई स्त्री है, जिसका पति उसका शृंगार देख कर सिर से पाँव तक जल उठे? ¶3 आज मुझसे ज़ब्त न हो सका। मैंने पूछा- तुमने मुझसे किस लिए विवाह किया था? यह प्रश्न महीनों से मेरे मन में उठता था, पर मन को रोकती चली आती थी। आज प्याला छलक पड़ा। यह प्रश्न सुनकर कुछ बौखला-से गये, बगलें झाँकने लगे, खीसें निकालकर बोले- घर सँभालने के लिए, गृहस्थी का भार उठाने के लिए, और नहीं क्या भोग-विलास के लिए? ¶1 Last night I fell asleep while reading the Bhaktamal. What great souls, for whom love of God was everything, absorbed in it always! Such devotion comes through hard discipline. ¶2 God, how shall I reason with my heart? You know every hidden thing, every fibre of me. I want to regard him as my chosen lord, serve his feet and obey his slightest sign, causing him not the least pain in word or deed. He is blameless; my fate brought this about. Neither he nor my parents are at fault, only my destiny. Yet knowing this, I feel my heart sink when he approaches, my face deaden and head grow heavy. I want neither his face nor conversation. Perhaps even seeing an enemy could not weary the heart so. At his arrival time my heart pounds. If he leaves for a day or two, a burden lifts: I laugh, speak and find some joy. News of his return darkens everything again. Why I feel so, I cannot say. Perhaps we were enemies in a former birth and he married me for revenge; old impressions remain. Otherwise why should he burn at seeing me and I detest his face? Surely this isn't marriage's meaning! I was happier at home and might have remained so all my life. Curse this social custom that insists on tying every unfortunate girl to some man's neck! Does it know how many young women weep over it, how many tender hearts surging with hopes it tramples? ¶4 Why does he suspect me so? Since fate brought me here, suspicion has coloured every glance. Why? If I braid my hair, he bites his lips. Coverage and translation notes

Narrator and extreme speech
The story is the unnamed woman's first-person account. Her despair, poison references and violent rhetorical appeal to parents in ¶28 are retained as her words, not recommendations. Her rejection of the former marriage and her final refusal to return are both preserved.
Ambiguity and inferred setting
¶8 prints स्वयं where the sentence's context points to स्वर्ग; English gives ‘heaven’ and flags the slip here. The closing account implies exploitation through the older woman's deception but describes no specific intervening acts; none are invented. The epithet कुलटा is represented as the narrative's harsh social label, not an independent judgement of her.
