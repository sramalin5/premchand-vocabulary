Coverage and translation notes

Source fidelity
All 63 prose paragraphs and source markers 2, 3 and 4 are retained. A sentence in the fire-jumping dialogue is split between ¶38 and ¶39 on the website; the translation retains that boundary rather than merging paragraphs.
Names and wordplay
Names are Halku, Munni and Sahna. Jabra, Jabru, Jabbar and the source’s spelling variants refer to the same dog. Halku’s name evokes lightness, explaining the narrator’s contrast with his large body.
Rural terms
हार means cultivated fields here. टाँठे and दंदाया are regional or unusual forms; contextual interpretations are noted rather than silently replacing the Hindi. Traditional months and the watch of the night are retained without imposed calendar dates.
Voice and tone
The coarse abuse directed at the west wind is rendered ‘wretched’. The intimate teasing of the dog and the final irony are retained. Statements about a neighbour’s comfort or the dog’s thoughts belong to Halku or the narrator, rather than added explanation.
