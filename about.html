Purpose

This site is for non-Hindi speakers who want to build their Hindi vocabulary and become more confident readers. Its main learning material is Munshi Premchand’s short stories: their characters, dialogue, and situations give unfamiliar words a memorable context.

The library brings original Hindi passages together with English translations, word meanings, idioms, and pronunciation comparisons. Read at your own pace, check how a word is used, then listen to the Hindi passage. The emphasis is vocabulary in meaningful sentences rather than isolated word lists.

Features and how to use them

Find a story

The library groups 142 illustrated stories into five reading levels. Search by Hindi or English title, open a level, and choose a story. Use Show all levels or Hide all levels to browse the collection.

Learn the script and numbers

The Devanagari, sounds & numbers page has clickable character charts, including consonants arranged in traditional groups. The ten-by-ten number chart combines tens on the left with units across the top, covering 0–99 with 100 below. Every number has Hindi and English names and Hindi audio. Use the player to pause, stop, or change speed.

Read Hindi with English

Each story has numbered Hindi paragraphs and a complete English translation. Paragraph-number links move between the Hindi and English versions. Top-of-page section links and previous/next links help you move through longer stories.

Choose Words or Phrases

Switch the highlighting mode to study individual words or idioms and phrases in both languages. Hover over a highlight, or focus it with the keyboard, to see its meaning and meaning in context. Click it to open its glossary entry.

Study the glossary

Words and idiom tables include meanings, source passages, explanations in context, and source references. Glossary headwords link back to their Hindi occurrences. Very simple vocabulary is excluded so the tables concentrate on useful learning material.

Compare similar sounds

The third Words column compares pronunciation, not synonyms. Hover over or focus a comparison word for its meaning and any available story context. Click it to open its Words-table entry. Added comparison entries identify the story supplying their example.

Control the reading view

Show or hide Words tables, Idioms & phrases tables, Original Hindi, and English translation independently. Following a glossary link can reveal a hidden destination, so you can return to the relevant entry.

Review all stories together

The combined vocabulary page has separate Words and Idioms & Phrases tables. Sort by word, story level, or story name. Both tables show the story’s reading level. The story dropdown lists each story’s level and offers Select all / Select none for each level, as well as for the whole list. The separate word-difficulty dropdown narrows Words independently. Tables show 50 entries per page.

Understand the levels

Story levels describe reading difficulty. The combined Words table has a separate, estimated difficulty rating for each word: 1 is most familiar and 5 is most advanced. The word-difficulty filter affects Words; the story filter affects both combined tables.

Every story also retains its Coverage and translation notes. These explain source coverage, translation choices, and expressions that need extra care.

Listen to selected Hindi

  1. Open a story and make sure Original Hindi is visible.
  2. Select a word, sentence, or passage in the original Hindi text. On a phone or tablet, press and hold, then adjust the selection handles.
  3. Click or tap the floating 🔊 Listen button. The selected text is read aloud.
  4. Click Stop to stop playback. On a keyboard, Esc stops playback and dismisses the button.

Usually, nothing needs installing. The page uses an available Hindi browser voice and automatically switches to its bundled Hindi voice if that voice is missing or fails. The bundled voice works without an online speech service, though it sounds more synthetic.

Selecting text shows the button; playback starts when you click Listen. This click also enables audio in browsers that restrict automatic playback. No microphone permission or speech extension is needed.

Browser setup

First try the steps above. If there is no sound, use the instructions for your browser. Change permissions for this site where possible; there is no need to enable sound everywhere.

Perplexity Comet
  1. Keep the story tab unmuted and turn up your device’s media volume. If the tab menu offers an unmute command, use it.
  2. If audio is blocked, open Comet Settings and search for Sound. Allow sound for the story page where that setting is available.
  3. Reload the story, select Hindi text, and click Listen. If the browser’s Hindi voice silently finishes immediately, the site automatically uses the bundled Hindi voice; no extension is required.

Browser help: Comet getting-started guide.

Google Chrome

In Chrome, open Settings → Privacy and security → Site settings → Additional content settings → Sound. Allow this page to play sound and unmute its tab if necessary. Reload, select Hindi, and click Listen. A browser Hindi voice is optional because the site has a bundled fallback.

Official help: Chrome sound permissions.

Microsoft Edge

Unmute the story tab. In Edge Settings, search for Sound and allow sound for this page if it is blocked. Select Hindi text and click the site’s Listen button. Edge’s separate Read Aloud command is not required.

For an optional system Hindi voice on Windows, follow the Windows voice instructions below. The bundled fallback also works when Edge has no Hindi browser voice.

Official help: Microsoft language and voice downloads.

Mozilla Firefox

Unmute the tab, select Hindi, and click Listen. If Firefox reports blocked audio, use its address-bar permissions to allow audio for this site. The relevant control is under Settings → Privacy & Security → Permissions → Autoplay → Settings; add a site exception if needed. The bundled voice can play when no Hindi browser voice is available.

Official help: Firefox audio and autoplay permissions.

Apple Safari on Mac

Unmute the story tab, select Hindi, and click Listen. If Safari blocks playback, check Safari → Settings → Websites → Auto-Play for the current site and allow playback if necessary. A click on Listen is normally enough to start this site’s audio.

A Mac Hindi voice is optional; see system voices. The bundled fallback is available if the browser voice cannot read the selection.

Official help: Safari site playback settings.

Brave

Open Settings → Privacy and security → Site & Shield Settings. Check Sound and, if playback is blocked, Autoplay for this site. Allow the page’s audio, then select Hindi and click Listen. Keep JavaScript enabled so the reading button and bundled voice can run.

Official help: Brave site permissions.

Opera

Unmute the story tab. Open Opera Settings and search for Sound, or find the page’s permissions under Privacy & security → Site settings. Allow sound where it is blocked, then select Hindi and click Listen. The site provides a Hindi fallback voice.

Official help: Opera website preferences.

Phones and tablets: Safari, Chrome, and other browsers

Turn up media volume and check the audio output, including connected Bluetooth headphones. Press and hold the original Hindi text, adjust the handles, then tap the page’s Listen button. Keep the story in the foreground while listening. If a preview or embedded browser suppresses selection or sound, open the full story in your regular browser.

On Android Chrome, check Settings → Site settings → Sound if sound is blocked. On iPhone or iPad, start playback with a tap on Listen. Menu names can vary by device and browser version.

Optional: add a system Hindi voice

Windows: open Settings, search for language settings, and add Hindi. Select its text-to-speech component where available, allow the download to finish, and restart the browser. Browser support for installed voices varies. Microsoft’s installation steps.

Mac: open System Settings → Accessibility and find Read & Speak (called Spoken Content on some versions). Use the system voice settings to look for a Hindi voice. Restart the browser after a voice download. Apple’s spoken-text guide.

These downloads are optional for this site: the bundled Hindi voice supplies a fallback.

If you still cannot hear the text

  • No button: select text in the original Hindi story, not the English translation or glossary table. Make sure JavaScript and Original Hindi are enabled.
  • Button stops immediately: refresh the page to load the latest reader. On Windows, use Ctrl + Shift + R. The updated reader detects an implausibly fast finish and switches voices.
  • “Preparing Hindi voice…”: let the bundled voice finish loading. If you moved or copied the library, keep its assets folder alongside the HTML pages.
  • Still silent: unmute the browser tab, check your device’s volume and output device, and allow sound for this page. Try a short sentence before a longer passage.
  • Using an app preview: try the same story in Comet, Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari. Some previews restrict audio or scripts.
