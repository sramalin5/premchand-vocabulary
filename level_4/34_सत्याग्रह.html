On one side streets were being cleaned and decorated with flags, and a pavilion was rising; on the other, soldiers and police drilled through streets and lanes with fixed bayonets. The officials strained every nerve to prevent the strike, while the Congress workers insisted that it must take place. If the officials relied on brute force, they relied on moral force. Let both be tested this time and see who held the field.

They will picket our shops, let their hair grow against us, throw themselves into wells, and fast. Who knows, a few may even give their lives, and our faces will be blackened forever. Your Honor would do us a great favor by reasoning with those Congress people. We lose nothing by refusing to strike. Great men from all over the country will come; with our shops open we could charge double and sell goods dearly. But what can we do? Coverage and translation notes

Satire and assertions
Boasts of ritual powers and threats about government conduct belong to the satire's characters. The officials plan to exploit religious fear; the translation preserves that plot without endorsing the claims.
Printed remnants
The source ends with a stray 1 and a separate line of dashes. They are retained as [1] and a separator rather than treated as missing prose. The unusual phrase about growing hair against shopkeepers is retained literally; its precise practice is not explained in the source.
Measures and food
A maund and a seer are traditional weight units; a lakh is 100,000. Amriti, laddu, rasgulla, mohanbhog, and kalakand are sweets. Kewra is fragrant floral water; harad is a traditional digestive ingredient.
