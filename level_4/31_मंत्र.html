He had no money—at least that was what people thought—but possessed priceless jewels of courage, patience, and intelligence, all offered to the Sabha. He was virtually the soul of 'purification.' The cure for all its moral, physical, mental, social, economic, and religious ailments was confined to the movement's success, and he worked for it body and soul. Chaube was an expert at collecting subscriptions. God had taught him the art of extracting oil even from stone. He shaved misers with such a blunt razor that they learned a lifelong lesson! He did not become as stout as before, but his agility and quickness doubled. Not a trace of the old fat-induced sluggishness remained. New life flowed through him.

Coverage and translation notes

Which Mantra
This is the story about Pandit Liladhar Chaube, identified by the site's slug mantra-2, rather than another story with the same Hindi title.
Religion and caste
Caste exclusions and religious hostility are the characters' or narrator's statements. 'Purification' renders shuddhi, the movement discussed in the source. The final mantra is the lesson of humane service, not an invented ritual formula.
Printed uncertainties
The final long paragraph prints मंत्र बनने लगे, literally 'mantras began to be made.' That unusual wording is retained rather than silently substituting 'temples.' The malformed सवा in the promised-reward passage is interpreted provisionally as spiritual merit. The source calls the illness nonfatal only after portraying it as plague; both statements are preserved.
Measures and allusions
Seer and dhela are traditional weight and coin units; a lakh is 100,000 and a kos a traditional distance unit. Izrail is the angel of death. Dadhichi and Harishchandra exemplify sacrifice; Hanuman's life-restoring herb is an epic allusion. These are narrative references, not medical claims.
