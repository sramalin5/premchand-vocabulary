On its right bank stands a mound, with an old fortress surrounded by wild trees. East of the mound is a little village. Both this stronghold and the village commemorate the glory of a Bundela lord. Centuries passed; many kingdoms rose and fell in Bundelkhand; Muslims came, and Bundela kings rose and fell. ¶2 Aniruddh Singh was a brave Rajput. Those were times when everyone relied solely on his own strength and valor. On one side Muslim armies stood firmly established; on the other, powerful kings were ready to strangle their weaker brothers. Coverage and translation notes

Names and wording
The source varies spellings of Orchha and Sarandha; English uses Orchha and Sarandha consistently, except that the printed Oraksha in the grant description is rendered Oraksha. Muhiuddin and Alamgir refer to the prince subsequently named Aurangzeb in the text.
Uncertainty in the source
The narrative calls Wali Bahadur Khan's body a corpse, but later presents him alive. This inconsistency is retained. Sarandha believes she has lost Chhatrasal; no missing account of his mission has been supplied.
Allusions and measures
Raghuvir is a name of Rama; Kaushalya is Rama's mother. The gold-adorned deer evokes a beautiful lure bringing disaster. A lakh is 100,000; kos is a traditional distance unit. The numerical military rank is retained without equating it with actual troop strength.
Narratorial attitudes
The passages praising martial honor and characterizing women are translated as the narrator's views, without rewriting them.
