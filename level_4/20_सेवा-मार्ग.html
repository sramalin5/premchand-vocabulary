¶1 For twelve years Tara practiced austerities for Durga. She neither slept on a bed nor arranged her hair nor put kohl in her eyes. She slept on the earth, wore ochre robes and ate dry bread. Her face was like a withered bud, her eyes without light, her heart an empty, desolate plain. Only one longing possessed her: to behold Durga. Her body melted like a candle, but that flame never left her heart. This was her wish, her life's purpose. ‘Will you spend your whole life weeping? The gods of these times are stone. ¶2 Thus twelve full years passed, and then the goddess was pleased. It was night; silence lay all around. A dim ghee lamp burned in the temple. Tara bowed her head at Durga's feet in true devotion. ¶3 Tara stood up. Her body trembled like the quavering sound of a peasant's song at dawn. She felt as though she were flying through the air. In her heart she sensed lofty thoughts and perfect light.
