Coverage and translation notes

Historical practice and the narrator's language
Sati here means a widow's death on her husband's funeral pyre. The translation retains the story's idealizing imagery and the narrator's praise as part of the original literary viewpoint, alongside the final account of four lives destroyed.
Rajnandini's inference
The final paragraph explains her suspicion, demand and resolve. The translation does not replace that explanation with an invented confession or affair: the earlier scene explicitly shows Dharm kneeling before Brajvilasini's sword.
Printed irregularities
The first paragraph has corrupted text in the country list, apparently Egypt; Egypt is tentative. कालिदास के देवता in ¶11 is also irregular, rendered divine poet Kalidasa from context. The Hindi remains exactly as cached from the site.
Names and titles
Durga Kunwari is called दुर्गाकुमारी in the final paragraph; the English uses one consistent name. Diwan is an administrative title. Meghaduta and Raghuvamsha are the Sanskrit works named in the source, not additional passages supplied from them.
