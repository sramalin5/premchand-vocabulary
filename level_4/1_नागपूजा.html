Coverage and translation notes

Supernatural fiction
Mother's explanations of snakes, the professor's remedies and his shifting theories belong to the story's supernatural world. They are preserved as beliefs and narrated events, without supplying a medical or zoological explanation the source does not give.
Printed irregularities
In ¶26 केशों literally means hair, though the surrounding precautions seem to describe coverings or floor cloths; coverings is tentative. In ¶32 मिथ्यावादी literally means one who speaks falsely, but the context suggests a turn towards superstition. The Hindi remains untouched.
The three marriages
The first groom dies before the bride departs, the second after the remarriage ceremony, and the third is Professor Dayaram. The separate ceremonies and deaths have not been merged.
The unresolved ending
The story ends with Tilottama's chest pain. It does not provide a final scientific solution or further account of the couple's life; none has been added.
