When, overcome with devotion, she sang her nectar-filled verses in her sweet voice at Ranchhorji's temple, her listeners became intoxicated with loving devotion. This ocean of love's nectar satisfied not only Chittor's inhabitants: it flooded the desert of all Rajputana.

¶2 Once it happened that both the Rao of Jhalawar and the prince of Mandar came to Chittor with their retinues. With the Rao was Princess Prabha, whose beauty and virtues were renowned far and wide. Tormented by separation, the princess peered from her palace windows. Both came anxiously to the temple at evening, and there the water lily bloomed on seeing the moon.

Coverage and translation notes

Honor and coercion
The title means ‘The Altar of Honor.’ The story portrays the pressures of abduction, public reputation and imposed marital fidelity. The characters' judgments are retained as their own.
A difficult battle sentence
Paragraph 28 says a hundred men lay wounded but that no one had raised a sword. The statement is retained without supplying an explanation absent from the source.
Time and a source slip
The narrative says two months have passed in paragraph 33, while the Rana speaks of one month in paragraph 38. Paragraph 85 prints ‘तुझे भय है’ where the speaker's sense appears to be ‘I fear’; the English follows that sense.
An unnamed beloved
At the end of paragraph 36 Prabha speaks of worshiping ‘him’ inwardly. The passage implies her beloved, but does not repeat his name; the pronoun is retained.
