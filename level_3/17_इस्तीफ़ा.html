Coverage and translation notes

The name's irony
Fateh means victory, while हार means defeat. The narrator's proposed Harchand turns Fatehchand's name into a joke about his repeated defeats before the final first victory.
What he tells Sharda
In ¶59 he invents a story of having struck back. The actual return with the borrowed staff occurs later, after Sharda's response. These two episodes are not merged.
Published repetitions and breaks
Paragraph 11 repeats his sitting and eager glance; the translation retains it. Sentences split between ¶19–20 and ¶91–92 remain split with connecting dashes.
Unclear speaker in ¶42
The printed paragraph assigns the opening to Fatehchand and immediately continues with the officer's abusive reply. The English keeps the question followed by the reply without attributing both to the clerk.
Time references
The later reference to an insult half an hour earlier is preserved alongside the earlier account of his journey and return home; no invented timing has been inserted.
