Coverage and translation notes

The salt wordplay
नमकहराम and नमकहलाली draw upon salt as a symbol of loyalty to one's employer or provider. In this story the same words also refer literally to the salt department. The magistrate's criticism of excessive loyalty to salt preserves that irony.
Names in the opening
Zulekha, Shirin and Farhad belong to Persian love-story traditions. The source prints सीरी for Shirin and uses the conventional expression discovery of America; both are translated in their apparent literary context without changing the Hindi.
Source irregularities
The printed phrases नमक से हलाली in ¶42, साकार in ¶36, वाषक in ¶59 and ठकुरसुहाती in ¶54 appear irregular. The translation follows the apparent contextual senses: loyalty, moneylenders, annual, and sycophancy. The Hindi remains exact.
Large sums
A lakh is one hundred thousand. The final offer is six thousand rupees annually, plus the separately listed provisions; the bribe escalation and salary are kept distinct.
